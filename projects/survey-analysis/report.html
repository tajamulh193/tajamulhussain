Key findings

  1. Manager support is the strongest driver: each extra point on the 5-point support scale goes with a +0.33 change in wellbeing, holding everything else constant (p < .001).
  2. Higher workload lowers wellbeing by 0.31 points per scale point (p < .001).
  3. Hybrid workers score 0.47 points higher than office workers (Welch t(327) = 7.14, p < .001, Cohen's d = 0.78).
  4. Departments differ only slightly and not significantly (F(3, 414) = 0.82, p = .485).
  5. Together the six predictors explain 40% of the variation in wellbeing (adjusted R² = 0.39).

Recommendation: invest in line-manager training and workload planning first. Together they matter more than where people work.

Method

Analysis in Python 3 using only the standard library. Test statistics and p-values (t, F, Pearson, OLS) are computed in stats.py and checked against SciPy and statsmodels in the automated tests. The same steps can be reproduced in SPSS, STATA or JAMOVI.
